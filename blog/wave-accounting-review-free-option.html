Wave accounting review: the best free option

Published May 3, 2026 · About 7 minutes

Wave is the rare product that admits it is free upfront without hiding the invoice behind a mystery phone call. For freelancers who need clean recurring invoices and basic bookkeeping, that honesty matters. Free still has a cost: time, attention, and the occasional feature gap when you grow faster than your spreadsheet habits.

I reach for Wave when someone is starting and cash is tight, not when they are optimizing tax strategy across three states and a holding company. It handles simple service businesses well. It handles chaos poorly, like any tool that expects you to categorize receipts before coffee.

What Wave does well

Invoicing is straightforward, and clients like paying with cards when it works. Recurring profiles save you from rewriting the same polite email with a PDF attached like it is 2009. The accounting side is real enough to keep you honest if you actually open it monthly.

The price tag helps you recommend a stack to beginners without sounding like you are recruiting them into a cult annual contract. That matters if you coach new freelancers or build starter kits for friends leaving full-time jobs.

Where it gets thin

Payroll and deeper reporting are paid add-ons or reasons to graduate. If you live inside integrations and automation, Wave will feel quieter than FreshBooks or QuickBooks culture. That is not a moral failure. It is positioning.

Support is not white glove. If you hate reading help docs, budget emotional resilience. Also remember free products change roadmap pace. Export your data periodically like a grown-up.

Verdict

Wave is the best free option for many solo freelancers who sell services and want respectable invoices without funding a SaaS collection. Pair it with discipline, not denial. Read our free invoicing hidden costs piece before you romanticize zero dollars forever.

Bank feeds and habits

Connecting bank feeds saves typing until it creates categorization laziness. Reconcile monthly like you mean it. Wave will not stop you from miscategorizing travel as snacks. Discipline still wins.

If you hire a bookkeeper later, exports and consistent categories become their love language. Build those habits while the transaction volume is still small enough to correct without tears.

Notebook

When clients ask which tool to buy first, I ask what task currently steals sleep. Software should shrink that task, not add meetings about software. I keep a rolling list of canceled subscriptions: tools that duplicated work, tools that promised automation but only moved chaos, tools that hid limits until renewal week. That list keeps recommendations honest because embarrassment is a decent teacher. If you are building your stack, write down the last three friction spikes you hit on real deadlines, then shop against those spikes instead of against landing page adjectives.

If you bill by the hour, also write down how many hours a year you spend re-learning tools you barely use. Multiply by your rate. That number is the honest budget for experimentation. Anything above it is hobby spending dressed as productivity. The Freelancer Stack exists to cut that waste without pretending one vendor saves your whole career.

What I would do next week

I would export a simple stack map: client intake, delivery surface, invoicing, backups, and security baseline. If two tools occupy the same box, pick a loser before next month. If a box is empty, decide whether that risk is real or imagined. Freelancers love imagining security problems while ignoring boring invoice gaps that actually cap growth.

I would also schedule one hour to read pricing and cancellation terms for anything that auto-renews above a hundred dollars a year. That hour pays for itself the first time you catch a price hike or a feature you stopped using in March. Boring maintenance beats heroic hustle every time.
